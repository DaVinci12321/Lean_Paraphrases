Among all quadruples of positive whole numbers in which the sum of the first two equals the sum of the last two, and twice the product of the first two equals the product of the last two, and the first is at least as large as the second, find the largest real number that is a lower bound for the ratio of the first to the second. Prove that the answer is $3 + 2\sqrt{2}$.
Suppose a real number is a root of a cubic whose value at any number is that number's cube plus a fixed real coefficient times the number plus a fixed real constant term; prove that four times the product of the constant term and that root is at most the square of the coefficient.
Consider those positive whole numbers that are divisible by each of sixteen, fifteen and fourteen, and for which one can find three different positive whole numbers, all smaller than fourteen, such that dividing the number by any one of these three leaves a remainder of exactly three; show that for the smallest such number, the sum of its distinct prime divisors is one hundred forty-eight.
Suppose four real numbers add up to two and the sum of all six of their pairwise products is zero; find the least and the greatest possible value of the product of the four numbers. Prove that the answer is that the least value is negative one and the greatest value is one quarter.
Prove that for every positive whole number one can always find two positive whole numbers such that the given number divides the quantity obtained by adding the square of twice the first to the square of three times the second and then subtracting one.
For any three positive real numbers, prove that the cyclic sum of the reciprocals of the quantity obtained by adding the product of all three numbers to the sum of the cubes of two of them is at most the reciprocal of the product of the three numbers.
Suppose a function assigns to every nonnegative integer a nonnegative integer in such a way that, for each nonnegative integer, its value at four times that integer is the sum of its values at twice that integer and at the integer itself, its value at two more than four times that integer is one more than its value at four times that integer, and its value at one more than twice that integer is one more than its value at twice that integer; prove that for every positive whole number, the count of nonnegative integers smaller than that power of two at which the function agrees on four times and three times the integer is exactly the function's value at the next power of two.
Find all positive whole numbers with the property that, whenever that many positive real numbers are chosen, the product of the sum of their squares with their sum, decreased by the sum of their cubes, is at least six times the product of all of them. Prove that the answer is $3$.
Given a positive integer $k$, find every ordered triple of positive integers whose sum is one more than three times $k$ and whose sum of the three pairwise products equals $k$ times the quantity two more than three times $k$. Prove that the answer is exactly the three orderings in which two of the numbers equal $k$ and the remaining one is $k+1$.
For each of the first fourteen positive integers, form the fraction whose numerator is the sum of that integer and the next one and whose denominator is the product of their squares; determine the total of these fourteen fractions. Prove that the answer is $\frac{224}{225}$.
Let $p$ be an odd prime, and let $n$ be one third of the number one less than two raised to the power twice $p$; prove that two raised to the power one less than $n$ leaves a remainder of one when divided by $n$.
Consider the quartic polynomial in one variable whose coefficients, from the fourth-degree term down to the constant term, are one, negative six, twenty-six, negative forty-six, and sixty-five, and suppose we are given four complex numbers, each of which has integer real part and integer imaginary part and each of which is a root of this polynomial; find the sum of the absolute values of the imaginary parts of these four numbers. Show that the answer is $10$.
Suppose two complex numbers have moduli two and nine respectively, and the modulus of the difference between five times the first one and the second one is also nine; find the modulus of the sum of five times the first one and the second one. Show that the answer is the square root of two hundred eighty-one.
Given a function $f$ from the natural numbers to the natural numbers, prove that the sum of any natural number and the value of $f$ at any natural number divides the value of $f$ at the first number plus the first number times the value of $f$ at the second, for every such choice of the two numbers, precisely when at each natural number the function takes either the square of that number or the value one.
Let $m$ and $n$ be positive whole numbers, with $m$ odd, and prove that one less than two raised to the $m$-th power is relatively prime to one more than two raised to the $n$-th power.
In a triangle whose three angles are each strictly between zero and a straight angle and together make a straight angle, suppose the two sides enclosing the third angle have lengths four and six, and suppose half the product of these two lengths with the sine of the angle they enclose equals six times the square root of two; prove that the enclosed angle is either half a right angle or one and a half right angles. Show that the answer is $45^{\circ}$ or $135^{\circ}$.
A sealed cone-shaped container is twelve inches tall and its circular base has a radius of five inches; held with its apex pointing down and its base level, the liquid inside stands nine inches deep, and held with its apex pointing up and its base level, the liquid's depth in inches is a positive whole number minus a positive whole multiple of the cube root of a positive whole number that is divisible by no prime cube. Find the sum of those three whole numbers. Prove that the answer is $52$.
Let n be a natural number that is at least two, and consider every pair of relatively prime positive integers, the first smaller than the second, in which the larger one does not exceed n while their sum does exceed n; prove that adding up the reciprocals of the products of these pairs gives exactly one half.
Suppose a positive real number is equal to the cosine of the acute angle whose tangent is that same number; prove that the square of this number is $\frac{-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.
Determine every pair made up of an integer and a rational number for which two more than that integer power of two equals the square of that rational number, and show that the only such pairs are the exponent negative two paired with either three halves or its negative, and the exponent one paired with either two or negative two.
Suppose a point in the plane has real coordinates such that its second coordinate is two fifths of its first, and the product of its two coordinates is ninety; show that its first coordinate is fifteen.
Suppose a real-valued quantity depending on a real time variable follows exponential change, meaning that at every real time its value equals a fixed real initial amount multiplied by the exponential of a fixed real rate times the time; if the value is one hundred at time four and ten at time eight, prove that at time twelve the value is one, so the answer is $1$.
Suppose $a$, $b$ and $c$ are positive integers such that the $b$-th power of $a$ divides the $c$-th power of $b$, and the $c$-th power of $a$ divides the $b$-th power of $c$; prove that the square of $a$ divides the product of $b$ and $c$.
Let $S$ be the sum of the common logarithms of all the proper divisors of one million, that is, of every positive divisor of one million except one million itself. Find the integer nearest to $S$. Prove that the answer is $141$.
Prove that for any two real numbers, their sum divided by the amount by which the sum of their squares exceeds their product is at most twice the square root of two divided by the square root of the sum of their squares.
Find how many positive integers have a square root that differs from the square root of one hundred by less than one. Show that the answer is $39$.
Suppose a real-valued function is defined on all the non-negative whole numbers and has the property that for each whole number greater than one at least one prime divisor of that number satisfies: the value at the number equals the value at the corresponding quotient minus the value at that prime; suppose further that the value at the 2014th power of two, plus the value at the 2015th power of three, plus the value at the 2016th power of five, equals 2013; prove that the value at the square of 2014, plus the value at the cube of 2015, plus the value at the fifth power of 2016, equals $\frac{49}{3}$.
Ten distinguishable people wish to form a committee of five of themselves by first electing a group of leaders numbering at least one and at most four, who then fill the remaining committee seats from the people not elected as leaders; counting two committees with the same members but different groups of leaders as different, find the number of ways the committee can be formed. Show that the answer is $7560$.
Taking a real variable, consider the fraction whose numerator is two more than the variable and whose denominator is the square root of the quadratic given by six plus ten times the variable plus twenty-five times the square of the variable, and prove that an antiderivative of this fraction is one twenty-fifth of that same square root, plus nine twenty-fifths of the natural logarithm of the sum of one, five times the variable, and the square root of one more than the square of one plus five times the variable, together with an arbitrary constant of integration.
Let $f$ be the real function that sends each real number to twice the product of its square with its sine, and find the twenty-seventh derivative of $f$. Suppose four angles, each strictly between zero and a right angle, are given, the first being strictly smaller than the second, the tangent of the third being the arithmetic mean of the tangents of the first two, and the secant of the fourth being the arithmetic mean of the secants of the first two; prove that the third angle is smaller than the fourth.
Suppose three positive numbers have product at most one; prove that the sum of their reciprocals is at least one more than six divided by their sum.

Prove that the principal inverse tangents of the reciprocals of three, five, seven, and eight add up to exactly half of a right angle.
A drawer holds two red socks, two black socks, two white socks, and some positive number of blue socks, and when two socks are taken out at random without replacement the chance that they match in colour is one fifth; find how many blue socks there are. Show that the answer is $4$.
Let $n$ be a natural number that is at least four, and consider two lists of $n$ nonnegative real numbers, paired up term by term, whose totals are equal to one another and positive; find the largest value that can be attained by the ratio whose numerator is the sum, over all positions, of the first list's entry times the sum of the two entries at that position, and whose denominator is the sum, over all positions, of the second list's entry times that same sum of the two entries, and prove that the answer is one less than $n$.
For every real number $x$, let $I(x)$ denote the integral, taken as $t$ runs from minus six tenths up to minus two tenths, of the product of two quantities: the first is two and one tenth times the fifth power of $t$ plus the sine of $t$, and the second is the exponential of $x$ times the quantity obtained by subtracting one and one tenth times the square of $t$ from one and one half times the cosine of $t$; prove that as $x$ grows without bound the ratio of $I(x)$ to minus twenty-seven hundredths times the exponential of one and forty-three hundredths times $x$, divided by $x$, tends to one, so show that the answer is $I(x)\approx-\frac{0.27e^{1.43x}}{x}$.
Divide a straight angle into seven equal parts; prove that the cosine of one such part, minus the cosine of two such parts, plus the cosine of three such parts, equals one half.
